## 1. Interpreted Rule-Based Multimodal Fusion for Emotion Recognition

**Conference:** III International Scientific and Practical Conference on Computer Science, Software Modeling, and Digital Systems Security (2026)

**Role:** First author

**Presentation type:** Oral presentation

**Abstract:**
This paper presents an interpretable rule-based emotion recognition system that integrates text, voice, and video modalities. Unlike black-box neural network approaches, the proposed fusion algorithm uses explicit rules, adaptive weighting, and operates in constant time O(1). The system architecture includes 18 REST API endpoints, JWT authentication with bcrypt hashing (12 rounds), and a normalized database with 9 tables.

**Key results:**
- Multimodal approach improves emotion classification accuracy by 17% compared to text-only analysis
- Detected 4 critical cases (9.1% of user messages) with zero false positives

## 3. Analysis of Dark Mode and Brutalism Aesthetics Impact on Emotional State and Cognitive Perception of Users

**Conference:** III International Scientific and Practical Conference on Computer Science, Software Modeling, and Digital Systems Security (2026)

**Role:** First author

**Presentation type:** Publication

**Abstract:**
This paper analyzes the impact of modern visual design paradigms — Dark Mode and Brutalism aesthetics — on cognitive perception, visual fatigue, and emotional state of users. The research identifies the limits of using these approaches depending on product specifics and target audience, while emphasizing the priority of adhering to international interface accessibility standards (WCAG 2.1).

**Key findings:**
- Dark Mode reduces visual fatigue in low-light conditions but can decrease readability for users with astigmatism
- Brutalism aesthetics create a distinctive visual identity but can increase cognitive load
- WCAG 2.1 compliance should remain a priority regardless of visual style

## 4. The Impact of Cyber Hackathons on Cybersecurity and Computer Networks Specialists Training

**Conference:** II International Scientific and Practical Conference on Computer Science, Software Modeling, and Digital Systems Security (2025)

**Role:** Co-author

**Presentation type:** Publication

**Abstract:**
This paper explores the role of cyber hackathons in training cybersecurity and computer networks specialists. Based on data from hackathons such as DEF CON CTF, Pwn2Own, and Hack The Box, the research demonstrates that interactive competitions are an effective tool for building professional competencies.

**Key results:**
- Participants in hackathons learn complex concepts 30–50% faster than students in traditional courses
- Capture The Flag (CTF) competitions develop practical problem-solving skills
- Hackathons bridge the gap between theoretical knowledge and real-world application
